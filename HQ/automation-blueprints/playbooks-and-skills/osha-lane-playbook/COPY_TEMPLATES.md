# COPY — the 3-step sequence pattern

Demand-side, reply-bait only. The job of this copy is the REPLY — not the meeting, not the sale. Rules that made it work: zero links, tracking off, 2–4 word noun-phrase subject, blank subjects on follow-ups so the thread holds, "Sent from my iPhone" signature, give-CTA not time-ask. Never name your client or reveal that you scraped anything — the recipient's own situation does the qualifying.

## Email 1 — subject: `Safety remediation`

```
Hey {{firstName}}

I have an EHS specialist taking on mid-size [REGION] plants with open OSHA citations right now — abatement through the re-inspection, so it stays closed.

[PROOF LINE — one sentence, one real quantified result from YOUR client. Ours: "They recently took a multi-site manufacturer through a repeat citation — an estimated $1.4M in shutdown and legal exposure cleared, inside 45 days." If you have no true number, OMIT this line. Never fabricate — one false claim kills the model.]

If anything's open on your side, would love to point them at it.

Best,
{{sendingAccountFirstName}}

Sent from my iPhone
```

## Email 2 — day +1, BLANK subject

```
Hey {{firstName}} — is resolving anything open with OSHA a priority for you right now?
```

## Email 3 — day +2, BLANK subject (breakup)

```
Leaving the door open. When it makes sense to get a citation closed properly, I'm one reply away.

Best,
{{sendingAccountFirstName}}

Sent from my iPhone
```

## Why each piece (evidence from real campaigns)

- **The quantified proof line is the single biggest lever.** Campaigns with one out-replied near-identical campaigns without one. It must be true.
- "I have… right now" = possession framing + present-tense urgency without fake scarcity.
- Email 2 is a yes/no question — the easiest possible thing to reply to.
- 40–60 words, one-line paragraphs, mobile-scannable.
- Cadence: 1-day delays. Stop on reply. Business hours, recipient-region timezone.
